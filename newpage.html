Influence & Rapport Mastery

You cannot lift another unless you are standing on higher ground. Learn to regulate your own nervous system, step into another person's world, and create the trust and responsiveness that moves people to action.

Inside This Session

Five practical skill areas — practised, not just discussed.

Self-Regulation Emotional Flexibility Match & Mirror Breaking Rapport

Step One: Regulate Yourself

You can't help anybody unless you yourself are feeling it. The first move in influence is never the other person — it's your own nervous system.

Box Breathing

In for 4 · hold for 4 · out for 4 · hold for 4. Three rounds is enough to reset.

  • Picturing the square keeps focus
  • Most effective when practised daily
  • Use before any high-stakes conversation

Three-Breath Reset

The quick version when you don't have time. In, out — three times, deliberately.

  • Works mid-conversation
  • Gets you out of your head, into your body
  • No one around you even notices

Cold Exposure

The fastest nervous-system reset available — cold water, cold plunge, ice bath.

  • Full reset in seconds
  • Not always practical — that's why breath comes first
  • Best used before, not during

Excitement Exercise

Jump up and down. Say "Yes!" out loud. Amp your energy on purpose.

  • Creates emotional flexibility
  • Called "emotional stretching"
  • Generates good feelings internally, not reactively

Emotional Flexibility

The skill of moving from one emotion to another — and back again — without missing a beat.

The Watchhouse

A law enforcement friend was calmly telling a story about taking up archery — the bow he bought, the arrows, his shots in the backyard.

Then an incident erupted in the background. He stood up and within moments was yelling at a prisoner, face red, wild, absolutely convincing. Anyone watching would swear he was furious.

A couple of minutes later the incident resolved. He sat back down and continued his archery story — from exactly the same place he left off, without missing a beat.

"That's when I realised he wasn't actually angry. He was matching and mirroring energy."

He had emotional regulation. He had emotional flexibility. He could access an emotion, use it for the period required, and put it away again.

What This Actually Means

You Become a Skilled Actor

Not losing the plot. Not losing your head. Not letting your nervous system take over and live in reaction. Instead: choosing the emotion you want to portray, accessing it, using it, and putting it away.

Regulated People Get More Done

In a panic state, the brain works slower, organising takes longer, and decisions get muddled. From a calm, regulated state you make fast, accurate decisions and give clear instructions — so you achieve more in less time.

Under Pressure, Deepen the Voice

In high-pressure situations the tendency is for voices to rise and speed up. The more effective move — for everyone — is to deepen the voice and slow the delivery. It's more commanding, and it works.

Step Two: Build Rapport

Once you're regulated, the goal is to get the other person to follow your lead. That begins with match and mirror.

Element 01

Pace

Match their speed of speech and movement.

Element 02

Tone

Match volume, pitch and emotional colour.

Element 03

Energy

Meet their intensity — don't dampen or inflate it.

Element 04

Posture

Reflect their stance, gestures and facial expression.

Repeat Their Words

Echo back key phrases and terminology. It signals focus and understanding — the way attorneys do it in negotiation, and the way great listeners do it everywhere.

Honest, Sincere Compliments

A genuine compliment brings the guard down and dissolves barriers. It must be real — people can tell the difference instantly.

Name the State

When someone says "I'm fine" but they're not, name what's underneath. "You've been a bit short with me — would I be right in thinking you're frustrated?"

The Detective Who Never Went to Court

Rapport beats task-orientation — every single time.

The Pattern

Other detectives were always in court. Their suspects dug their heels in, pleaded not guilty, and made every case harder than it needed to be.

Bill's Approach

He spent an hour just talking with the person. Showing empathy. Building rapport. Finding out about their life, their situation, what they were dealing with.

The Result

By the end, they felt like they were talking to a friend — someone who had their best interests at heart. He was sending them to court, and they were thanking him for it. No fights. No delays. No trial.

The Warrant Pickup

The same principle applies in the field. Picture a law enforcement officer with a warrant, needing to influence someone to get into the car.

The approach: "It's a weekday. If you come with me now, we can get this sorted in a few hours — court, back out, home before you know it. If you wait, we have to pick you up on a Saturday, and you could be in until Monday."

"Do you really want that? Come with me now, we'll deal with it, and it'll be over."

People complied. Time after time. Not because they were forced — because they were influenced. Rapport and clear framing beat antagonism every time.

The deeper lesson: the officer genuinely wanted the best outcome for that person. Compliance was a by-product of care.

Breaking Rapport — Yes, It Matters Too

Building rapport gets you trust and responsiveness. Breaking it brings attention back.

When You Need to Leave

Some conversations will go all day if you let them. Breaking rapport lets you exit respectfully — without signalling that the person was uninteresting.

When Attention Drifts

A clap, a loud noise, a sudden shift in energy. Teachers do it in classrooms. You do it with your kids. It works because it interrupts the pattern.

How to Do It Well

Bring in a different energy, speed or pace. Don't insult, don't criticise. Maintain the relationship while changing the dynamic.

Get Out of Your Head, Into Your Heart

People don't care how much you know until they know how much you care.

The Lost Luggage Trap

Someone tells you about a trip where their bag went missing. You immediately think of the time yours went missing too.

Suddenly you're telling your story — and yours was worse. Three days without luggage. You've one-upped them.

"You've left their story. You've taken the baton and run with it. They no longer feel heard."

That's the Me Monster. It's the default setting for most of us — and it destroys rapport in a single sentence.

Instead, Stay in Their World

Brief acknowledgement, then keep the focus on them: "That's rough. How long did it take to get it back? Tell me more — that must have been so frustrating."

Sympathy vs. Empathy

Sympathy comes from the lips out. Empathy comes from the heart out. Empathy is walking in their shoes — stepping into the scenario as them.

Dialogue, Not Monologue

Once rapport is built, you can share. But it's a two-way street. A conversation where one person talks and the other just nods isn't connection — it's performance.

What You'll Take Away

The fundamentals, distilled.

Regulate First, Always

You can't lift another unless you're on higher ground. Start at the bottom rung of the ladder — yourself.

Build Emotional Flexibility

Practise moving between emotional states on purpose. Breath work, box breathing, excitement exercises.

Match Pace, Tone, Energy, Posture

Meet people where they are before you try to move them anywhere else.

Don't Fix — Connect

Asking questions turns you into a coach. Matching and mirroring keeps you in their world.

Name What's Underneath

Read the emotion behind the words and reflect it back. That's how people feel truly understood.

Know When to Break Rapport

Bring in a different energy to reclaim attention or exit gracefully — without damaging the relationship.

Kill the Me Monster

Resist the urge to tell your own version. Stay in their story. All about them, not about you.

Influence Serves Contribution

The two needs of the spirit are growth and contribution. Influence is how you give beyond yourself.

Practise This in Your World

Influence is a skill — it develops through repetition, not theory. Take one tool from this session and use it today. Then come back and tell us what happened.
